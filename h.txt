The grease on Ignatius’s knuckles was standard brass-and-coal residue, but the sparks bouncing off his socket wrench were entirely illegal.

As the Royal Aether-Engineer, Ignatius didn't just build machines; he bound them. In the belly of the capital’s central clocktower, he wiped a smudge of soot from his goggles and adjusted the grand celestial flywheel. The city above believed the brass giant ran on steam and intricate gearwork. They didn't need to know about the captured elemental humming inside the central piston, frantically pedaling to keep time moving forward.

"Pressure's spiking," muttered Pip, his humunculus apprentice, who was currently hanging upside down from a copper pipe. "If the mana-manifold blows, we aren't just looking at an explosion, Iggy. We’re looking at next Tuesday happening yesterday."

"Hand me the enchanted torque wrench," Ignatius grunted, sliding beneath the main drive shaft.

The clockwork titan roared, a rhythmic thump-clack that rattled the rivets in his leather apron. A hairline fracture was spreading across the primary arc-reactor. Raw violet magic hissed through the crack, singing the hem of his trousers.

Ignatius didn't chant in ancient High Elven. He didn't wave a wand of polished oak. Instead, he pulled a blowtorch from his utility belt, channeled a stream of pyromantic energy through the butane nozzle, and began welding the rift shut. He scribed containment runes directly into the molten brass with the tip of a titanium screwdriver, whispering equations of mass, momentum, and arcane warding under his breath.

The violet glow flickered, fought back, and finally settled into a steady, submissive blue. The flywheel's frantic squeal smoothed into a deep, purring thrum.

Ignatius pulled himself up, took off his goggles, and took a long sip of lukewarm, over-steeped tea. The bells above began to chime noon, perfectly on cue. Science was reliable, but magic made it punctual.
